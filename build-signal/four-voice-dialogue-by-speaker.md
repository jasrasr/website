# Four-person dialogue: speaker cue sheet

This is the **labeled production copy**. The matching `four-voice-dialogue-upload.txt` omits names so ElevenLabs does not accidentally speak them. Read turns in numerical order; assign the named voice to each paragraph in Studio. The characters are fictionalized roles, not instructions to imitate a particular performer's voice.

## Jimmy Glick — American male, theatrical talk-show host

1. [annoyed] Why are they still here? The story ended. Even the footer has finished being a footer. And yet, our guest remains!
5. They can hear you, Roger. You are talking into their browser. This is a talk show, not a private meeting.
9. Ladies and gentlemen, why did the website need therapy? Too many unresolved issues! Thank you. I'll be here until somebody closes the tab.
13. What if they're waiting for a secret button? Every long page has one. Or is that just what my producers tell me?
17. New theory: they're checking whether the four voices sound different. Hello! This is your host, Jimmy, live from the bottom of the page.
21. Maybe they're inspecting the build process instead of auditioning us. In that case, our next guest has one useful answer!
25. This was supposed to be a talk show. It is becoming a very small conference about responsible deployment.
29. What if the viewer is Jason? What if he's checking whether we followed the instructions and kept the story honest? A surprise inspection!
33. So they're not stuck? They can leave, stay, or replay Laura's terrible joke and decide it was a bug report?

## Laura — female, enthusiastic and quirky

2. [brightly] I don't know! Maybe they like the glowing lines. Maybe they made a sandwich shaped like a tiny laptop.
6. [laughing] Let's tell them a joke! Something weird and wonderful, so they leave on a high note.
10. [giggling] You stole my joke and somehow made it sound like a maintenance notice. It deserved a drum roll and confetti.
14. I looked! There's a tour button, a signal map, and a voice selector. The mystery is regular navigation wearing dramatic lighting.
18. And this is Laura! A little warmth, a little sparkle, and absolutely no promise that Jimmy's joke improves with repetition.
22. Jason starts with an idea, asks for help, and still checks what changed. The tools don't make the final call. That's the trick.
26. [laughing] We can do both! Responsible facts first, ridiculous theory second. What if the viewer is a cat walking across the keyboard?
30. Then he'll notice we can't see who's here. This is theater, not surveillance. The page isn't watching the viewer.
34. [warmly] Exactly! Maybe they stayed because they're curious. That's a lovely reason. Thanks for making it all the way down here.

## Charlie — Australian male, deep, confident and energetic

3. [confidently] They can stay as long as they like. I checked twice, and I cannot find a closing time.
7. Hang on. We don't know how long they've been here. We only know they made it to this part. Good effort, honestly.
11. [energetically] And it deserved a passing test. An unresolved issue isn't a personality trait, mate. It's a ticket with a due date.
15. No secret exit, but there are seven chapters, and the last one is us. We're the post-credits scene. That's a solid finish.
19. Charlie here. The sound depends on the voices you choose. Pick a good one; give the story some energy.
23. GitHub keeps the source and history. Hosting may sync selected files. A health check proves a page answered, not which revision answered. Keep that straight.
27. A cat could scroll, but it couldn't compare source, hosting handoff, and monitoring. Probably. I've met some sharp cats.
31. And he'll notice the ending needs a human voice. We can riff around the edges, but Jason gets the final word. Fair enough.
35. Right, here's the finish: stop talking over the builder, leave one beat of quiet, and let his recorded voice play.

## Roger — American male, laid-back, casual and resonant

4. [casually] They haven't clicked away. Let them hang out. We aren't paying rent on this tab.
8. [laid-back] Someone's here. That's cool. No need to stare at the text like it owes you money.
12. [casually] Easy, everyone. They might be waiting for another joke. If we give them one, we'll be here all night.
16. [casually] Don't tell them that. They'll wait for a post-post-credits scene, and I left my script at home.
20. [relaxed] Roger here. I was hoping the microphone would forget me. So far, no luck. I'm good either way.
24. [casually] See? Useful and still relaxed. Also, keep a backup. I like knowing where the exits are.
28. [casually] I knew a focused cat once. Sat on a backup drive for an hour. Nice cat; terrible backup strategy.
32. [relaxed] Good. I'm out of theories anyway. Maybe the viewer is still here, waiting for that line.
36. [casually] Guys, just be patient. This too will pass. Go to sleep. Or stay. I'm not in charge of the tab.
